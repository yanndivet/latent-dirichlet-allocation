# Latent Dirichlet Allocation

An implementation of document topic models in Python, covering a Dirichlet-Multinomial
baseline, Bayesian Mixture Models, and Latent Dirichlet Allocation, all trained via
Gibbs sampling on a corpus of political blog posts.

Coursework 3 of 3 of Carl Edward Rasmussen's [4F13 course](https://mlg.eng.cam.ac.uk/teaching/4f13/2425/)
at the University of Cambridge (Part IIB Engineering Tripos). Awarded First Class.

---

## Overview

Standard bag-of-words models treat a document as an unordered sample from a single
multinomial over a vocabulary. Topic models go further by assuming each document is
generated from a mixture of latent topics, each of which is itself a distribution over
words. This repo builds up that idea in three stages: starting from a Dirichlet-Multinomial
baseline, adding mixture structure with a Bayesian Mixture Model (BMM) where every
document belongs to exactly one topic, and finally implementing full Latent Dirichlet
Allocation (LDA) where each word token is drawn from its own latent topic. Both mixture
models are fitted via collapsed Gibbs sampling and evaluated by perplexity on a held-out
test set from the Daily Kos 2004 U.S. election blog corpus.

## Key Ideas Explored

**Dirichlet-Multinomial baseline** A single multinomial over the vocabulary with a
symmetric Dirichlet prior yields a closed-form predictive distribution. Its perplexity
on the test set sets the bar that richer topic models must clear to justify their
added complexity.

**Bayesian Mixture Model (bmm.py)** Each document is assigned to exactly one of K
clusters. The Gibbs sampler removes a document from its current cluster, recomputes
the posterior probability of each cluster using the Dirichlet-Multinomial likelihood,
and resamples the assignment. Convergence is diagnosed by tracking mixing proportions
across sweeps — most of the 20 components collapse, revealing that only a handful of
topics carry meaningful mass.

**Latent Dirichlet Allocation (lda.py)** Rather than one topic per document, LDA
places a Dirichlet prior over topic proportions within each document and a Dirichlet
prior over word distributions for each topic. The collapsed Gibbs sampler marginalises
out both sets of Dirichlet parameters and resamples each word token's topic assignment
conditioned on all other assignments. Topic coherence is measured by word entropy: a
low-entropy topic concentrates probability on a small, thematically coherent set of words.

**Perplexity comparison** LDA (≈1701) substantially outperforms the BMM (≈2117) and
the bag-of-words baseline (≈2684) on the held-out Daily Kos test set. The gap reflects
LDA's mixed-membership assumption — a document about both economic policy and the Iraq
war can draw words from both topics simultaneously rather than being pinned to one cluster.
